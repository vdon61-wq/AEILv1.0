# AEIL Certification Policy — v1.0

**Status:** Public policy draft

## 1. Certification is separate from licensing

A person or organization may use, implement, modify, and redistribute AEIL materials under
the applicable licenses without receiving AEIL certification.

Certification is a separate conformity-assessment process.

## 2. Certification terminology

Unless formally authorized, implementations should not describe themselves as:

- AEIL Certified
- Official AEIL Certified
- AEIL Approved
- Official AEIL Approved

A project may instead use factual wording such as:

> Implements AEIL v1.0

or:

> Evaluated against the public AEIL Benchmark v1.0

provided that the statement is accurate.

## 3. What a certification record should identify

An AEIL certification record should identify, at minimum:

- AEIL specification version;
- benchmark version;
- evaluated model, agent, or system;
- relevant configuration/version;
- evaluation date;
- evaluation scope;
- score or result according to the applicable methodology;
- evaluator or certification body;
- validity period, if one is defined;
- applicable limitations.

## 4. No permanent guarantee

Certification describes performance under a defined evaluation scope and date. It should
not be represented as a permanent guarantee of future behavior, safety, reliability,
or regulatory compliance.

Changes to the model, prompts, tools, policies, retrieval systems, agent workflows,
deployment environment, or other material components may require reassessment.

## 5. Independent evaluation

The AEIL ecosystem may support independent auditors. Their reports should clearly
distinguish:

1. the AEIL methodology;
2. the auditor's testing process;
3. the evaluated system's results;
4. any auditor-specific conclusions.

No independent auditor should imply official AEIL certification unless authorized under
the project's certification governance.

## 6. Future governance

Future versions may define:

- certification levels;
- auditor accreditation;
- certification validity periods;
- surveillance/reassessment;
- badge requirements;
- public certification registry;
- dispute and appeals procedures.

Until such rules are formally published, this document should be treated as a policy
framework rather than a claim that a fully operational accreditation system already exists.

**Legal note:** This is a project policy template and should be reviewed by qualified
legal counsel before being used as a formal certification or trademark program.
